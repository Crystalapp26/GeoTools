LEARN GIS

Practical GIS guides

These short explainers introduce the ideas behind the GeoTools calculators. Use them as a starting point and check the assumptions before using results in research or professional work.

1. How to calculate distance between coordinates

Latitude and longitude are angular coordinates, not flat map distances. GeoTools uses the Haversine formula to estimate the great-circle distance between two points on a spherical Earth.

Formula: a = sin²(Δφ/2) + cos(φ₁) cos(φ₂) sin²(Δλ/2); d = 2R atan2(√a, √(1−a)), where φ is latitude in radians, λ is longitude in radians, and R is the chosen Earth radius (about 6,371 km).

This is a straight-line surface distance, not road or walking distance. For high-precision surveying or long-distance geodesy, use an appropriate ellipsoidal method.

2. What NDVI measures

The Normalized Difference Vegetation Index contrasts near-infrared (NIR) and red reflectance: NDVI = (NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red), when the sum is not zero. Values usually fall between −1 and +1 for common reflectance inputs. Higher positive values often indicate greener vegetation, but soil, water, atmosphere, sensor characteristics and processing affect interpretation.

Use surface reflectance when appropriate, check band selection and scale factors, and avoid comparing images acquired under different conditions without considering those differences. A single NDVI value does not uniquely identify land cover or vegetation health.

3. Decimal degrees and DMS coordinates

Decimal degrees store an angular coordinate as one decimal number, such as 55.8642°. Degrees-minutes-seconds (DMS) split the absolute value into degrees, minutes and seconds, while direction is shown by N/S for latitude and E/W for longitude.

To convert the fractional part, multiply it by 60 to get minutes; multiply the remaining fractional minutes by 60 to get seconds. Preserve the sign through the correct direction indicator, and check that latitude is between −90 and +90 and longitude between −180 and +180.

4. First checks before analysing a CSV

Before analysis, confirm the delimiter, column headings, missing values, units, date formats and whether numeric-looking columns contain text. Row counts and means are useful first checks, but they do not prove that a dataset is accurate or representative. For large or important datasets, use a validated analysis workflow and retain a copy of the original file.
